# The Chosen Word — OT Quotation Catalog

**Last updated:** 2026-10-22
**Status:** Draft — Phase 1, Loops 1–2 + D&C added
**Total entries:** 108 — 91 Gospels/3 Nephi + 17 D&C (separate section)

Source constraint: All scripture text verified against churchofjesuschrist.org. Speaker filter applied: Christ only. Narrator fulfillment formulas excluded.

**D&C note:** The D&C section is kept separate from the Gospels/3 Nephi entries. Speaker attribution in D&C is based on section-heading identification and first-person pronoun use. Five D&C entries are classified "tentative" — see confidence column. The D&C material represents latter-day revelation, not the mortal ministry, and should be displayed distinctly on the site.

### Passages requiring additional verification or judgment

**Q-056 (Luke 10:27–28):** Edge case. The lawyer, not Jesus, speaks the Deut 6 / Lev 19 text. Jesus affirms it. Included because the affirmation is the central teaching act, but marked for Chase to review regarding the speaker-filter rule.

**Q-057 (Luke 13:27 / Ps 6:8):** The parallel between "depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity" and Ps 6:8 is real but the allusion may be loose enough to reconsider. Retained because the wording is specific, not merely thematic.

**Q-068 (John 7:38 / Isa 55:1):** Jesus says "as the scripture hath said" but no single OT text is a close verbal match. The composite allusion is retained because Jesus explicitly invokes "the scripture," indicating he has a specific text in mind, even if scholars debate which one.

**Q-015 (Matt 12:40 / Jon 1:17):** The Church footnote for this verse cited Jon 3:5 rather than Jon 1:17. The three-days-in-belly language is in Jon 1:17; Jon 3:5 concerns Nineveh's fasting response. Footnote may be pointing to the repentance theme rather than the narrative source. The allusion to Jon 1:17 remains well-supported by the text itself.

**John 12:38–40:** Excluded. These are the narrator's fulfillment formulas citing Isaiah, not Jesus's own speech. Matthew 12:18–21 (Isa 42 servant quotation) is also the narrator's voice and was correctly excluded from this catalog.

**Luke 4:25–27 (Elijah/Elisha narratives):** Classified as close verbal allusion because Jesus invokes specific named OT narrative events (Zarephath widow, Naaman). No OT wording is reproduced. If Chase prefers to restrict the catalog to verbal parallels only, this entry could be reclassified as "narrative citation" and noted separately.

**3 Ne 21:14–18 (Micah 5 extended):** The BoM text differs in some details from Mic 5:10–14 KJV wording. Flagged for future comparison study.

### Classification decisions

- "Paraphrase with citation formula" is used when Jesus introduces an OT law with "ye have heard that it was said" or "it is written" but his wording is a summary, not a verbatim reproduction. This applies to all six Sermon on the Mount antitheses in Matt 5 and 3 Ne 12.
- "Direct quotation" is used when the words Jesus speaks closely match the KJV OT text, with or without a citation formula.
- "Close verbal allusion" is used when no citation formula is present and the wording is closely specific enough to identify a single OT source, but is not reproduced word-for-word.
- Narrator fulfillment formulas ("that it might be fulfilled") are excluded throughout, even when they quote scripture accurately.
