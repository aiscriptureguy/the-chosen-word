Per workflow Section 4.2 and 8.4, these are internal-motive claims that should be phrased as "one possible reason" or "this may indicate." ## 7. Three D&C Contributions Absent from Gospels and 3 Nephi

These three catalog items are unique to the D&C section and represent content that cannot be found in the mortal-ministry record. They deserve prominent placement in the study and on the site.

**1. Isaiah 63:1, 63:3 — "I have trodden the wine-press alone" (DC-015, DC-016 / D&C 133:47, 50–51)**
Christ quotes Isaiah 63's wine-press imagery in first person to describe his Second Coming and, by implication, his atoning suffering. This is the only place in the full 108-entry catalog where Christ speaks in his own voice about the cost of the Atonement using OT language. The Isaiah 63 passage was written centuries before Christ's birth; in D&C 133 he claims it as his own experience.

- Doctrinal status: Verified against D&C 133 on churchofjesuschrist.org. High confidence.
- Site note: This entry should be highlighted as distinctive — Christ citing Isaiah about himself in a way that is different in kind from citing Isaiah as prophecy or instruction.

**2. Zechariah 13:6 — "What are these wounds in thine hands?" (DC-009 / D&C 45:51–52)**
Christ quotes and answers Zechariah's wounded-shepherd question in first person at the moment of the Jews' recognition of him. No Gospel records this exchange; 3 Nephi does not contain it. This is the only entry in the catalog where Christ directly identifies himself as the wounded figure described by a specific OT prophet, in the presence of the people the prophecy was written for.

- Doctrinal status: Verified against D&C 45 on churchofjesuschrist.org. High confidence.
- Site note: This entry stands alone in the catalog as Christ explicitly connecting a specific wound prophecy to himself in a recognition context. Worth a dedicated passage record in a future loop.

**3. Ezekiel named explicitly — D&C 29:21 (DC-004)**
"According as it is spoken by the mouth of Ezekiel the prophet." This is the only entry across all 108 catalog entries where Christ names an OT prophet by name within the quotation formula itself. Every other explicit citation in the catalog names the text ("it is written") or the author generally ("Moses said," "Esaias"); this one names both the book and the prophet explicitly in the same clause.

- Doctrinal status: Verified against D&C 29 on churchofjesuschrist.org. High confidence.
- Site note: This uniqueness should be noted in the catalog display — it is the only time Christ cites by explicit prophet name in this catalog.
