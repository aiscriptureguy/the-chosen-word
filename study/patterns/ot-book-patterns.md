## 2. OT Books by Setting

### 2a. Wilderness — Satan's temptations (Matt 4 / Luke 4)

| Books used | Entries |
|-----------|---------|
| Deuteronomy only | Q-001–003, Q-049–051 |

**[OBS]** Every wilderness response draws from Deuteronomy and only Deuteronomy. The three citations are Deut 8:3, 6:16, and 6:13 — all from Moses's farewell covenant speech reminding Israel where ultimate loyalty lies.

**[INTERP]** Deuteronomy may function for Christ as the foundational covenant statement — the clearest articulation of what God requires above all. When the question is about ultimate allegiance (bread vs. word of God, testing God vs. trusting him, earthly kingdoms vs. worship of God alone), he returns to this covenant text. Tentative.

---

### 2b. Sermon on the Mount / Sermon at Bountiful (Matt 5 / 3 Ne 12)

| Books used | Entries |
|-----------|---------|
| Exodus | Q-004, Q-005, Q-008, Q-073, Q-074, Q-077 |
| Deuteronomy | Q-006, Q-075 |
| Leviticus | Q-007, Q-009, Q-076, Q-078 |
| Numbers | Q-007, Q-076 (paired with Lev 19:12) |

**[OBS]** All six antitheses draw from the Pentateuch. No Psalms, no Isaiah, no prophets. The OT sources are the recognized legal texts of Israel — commandments and ordinances most Jews would know by heart.

**[OBS]** The rhetorical structure requires recognized, authoritative law as the "before" state. Christ says "ye have heard that it was said" — the force depends on quoting something the audience unquestionably accepts. The Pentateuch law accomplishes this; a psalm or prophet would not carry the same legal weight in this rhetorical move.

### 2c. Hostile Disputants — Pharisees, Scribes, Sadducees, Lawyers

**David's son question:**
| OT Source | Entries | Books |

| OT Source | Entries | Books |

**[OBS]** Against hostile questioners, Christ draws from the widest variety of OT books: Hosea, 1 Samuel, Numbers, Exodus, Isaiah, Genesis, Deuteronomy, Leviticus, Psalms, Jonah. He does not restrict himself to one portion of the OT when answering opponents.

**[OBS]** A recurring pattern: when disputed about the law, Christ often cites a source his questioners had not considered or had missed. Against the Sabbath accusation, he cites David's own violation of temple law (1 Sam 21) and priestly temple service (Num 28). Against the tradition dispute, he cites a prophetic condemnation of the same attitude (Isa 29:13). Against the resurrection denial, he cites the burning bush (Exod 3:6) — a text they knew well but had not read carefully.

**[INTERP]** Christ's strategy with hostile questioners appears to be: meet them on their own textual ground, then show them what they missed or misread. He rarely invents a new framework; he uses their own canon to expose the gap in their reading. Tentative.

### 2d. Private Disciples

**Parables explanation:**
| OT Source | Books |
|-----------|-------|
| Isa 6:9-10 | Isaiah |

**Witness principle for dispute resolution:**
| OT Source | Books |
|-----------|-------|
| Deut 19:15 | Deuteronomy |

**Stumbling block teaching (Mark 9):**
| OT Source | Books |
|-----------|-------|
| Isa 66:24 | Isaiah |

**Olivet Discourse:**
| OT Source | Books |
|-----------|-------|
| Dan 9:27, 11:31, 12:11 | Daniel |
| Dan 7:13 | Daniel |

**[OBS]** With private disciples, Daniel appears — and only with disciples. The two Daniel citations (abomination of desolation, Son of Man in clouds) both appear in the Olivet Discourse, which is given exclusively to the inner circle.

**[INTERP]** Daniel's eschatological content may have been considered too advanced or too easily misunderstood for public teaching. Christ apparently reserved it for those prepared to hear it. Tentative — could also reflect the text's inherent difficulty rather than a deliberate audience restriction.

### 2e. Last Supper and Farewell Discourse (Private — disciples)

| OT Source | Entries | Books |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| Zech 13:7 | Q-031, Q-047 | Zechariah |
| Isa 53:12 | Q-065 | Isaiah |
| Ps 41:9 | Q-071 | Psalms |
| Ps 35:19 / 69:4 | Q-072 | Psalms |

**[OBS]** The Last Supper and Farewell Discourse contain four distinct OT citations, all from Isaiah, Zechariah, and Psalms. None from Deuteronomy, Exodus, or the legal texts.

**[OBS]** The Psalm citations here (Ps 41:9, Ps 35:19) are personal and predictive — about betrayal and hatred. Christ uses them to prepare his disciples for what is about to happen to him.

**[INTERP]** In his final hours with the Twelve, Christ appears to shift from instructional law texts to personal prophetic texts — scriptures that describe his experience and mission rather than commands or standards. Tentative.

### 2h. Nephite Multitude (3 Nephi)

**Sermon at Bountiful (same structure as Sermon on the Mount):**
Same OT books as the Galilean sermon — Exodus, Deuteronomy, Leviticus, Numbers.

**Extended gathering discourse (3 Ne 20-21):**
| OT Source | Books |
|-----------|-------|
| Isa 52 (multiple passages and verses) | Isaiah |
| Deut 18:15-19 | Deuteronomy |
| Gen 12:3 + 22:18 | Genesis |
| Mic 5:8, 5:10-14 | Micah |

**Malachi dictated (3 Ne 24-25):**
| OT Source | Books |
|-----------|-------|
| Mal 3 (full chapter) | Malachi |
| Mal 4 (full chapter) | Malachi |

**[OBS]** Isaiah completely dominates the 3 Nephi gathering discourse — more than any other setting in the catalog. Isaiah 52 alone is quoted more times in 3 Nephi than any other single OT chapter anywhere in the catalog.

**[OBS]** The Malachi chapters are explicitly presented as new material the Nephites had not received. Christ gives a reason for quoting them at length: "these scriptures which ye had not with you" (3 Ne 26:2, surrounding context). This is the only time in the catalog where Christ explains why he is quoting at length.

## 3. Summary — Book-Level Patterns

| OT Book | Primary Settings | What It Does for Christ |
|---------|----------------|------------------------|
| Deuteronomy | Wilderness (exclusively), Sermons, some disputes | Covenant foundation; ultimate allegiance; the known law as baseline |
| Isaiah | Disputes (some), Last Supper, Cross, Nazareth, 3 Nephi (extensively) | Prophetic identity; mission; future gathering; his own suffering |
| Psalms | Jerusalem temple, Last Supper, Trial, Cross | Personal voice; suffering and vindication; messianic identity |
| Exodus | Sermons, disputes, Rich Ruler | The law as recognized standard; the commandments everyone knows |
| Daniel | Olivet Discourse, Trial only | Eschatology; the Son of Man; reserved for high-stakes moments |
| Hosea | Sabbath disputes (Matthew only) | Diagnostic: what Pharisees missed about God's priorities |
| Genesis | Divorce dispute, Abrahamic covenant | The original order of creation; the foundational covenant promise |
| Malachi | John the Baptist, Nephite dictation | Preparation for the Lord's coming; end-times housekeeping |
| Zechariah | Last Supper only | The shepherd and scattering — Christ's own arrest prefigured |

## 4. Open Questions for Future Loops

- Does Christ's shift from Pentateuch (legal) texts to Psalms/Isaiah as the Passion approaches reflect a deliberate rhetorical movement — from teaching to testimony?
- Why does Daniel appear only in private/trial contexts? Is there evidence in official Church sources that sheds light on this?
- The Sermon at Bountiful exactly replicates the OT-book pattern of the Galilean Sermon on the Mount. Is this simply the same content delivered twice, or does the Nephite setting add a different covenant dimension that future loops should examine?
- Are there OT books entirely absent from Christ's recorded citations? Notably missing: Proverbs, Job, Ruth, Esther, Ezra-Nehemiah, Chronicles, Lamentations, Obadiah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai. Whether this absence is meaningful or simply a function of the record is an open question.
