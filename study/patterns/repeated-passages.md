# The Chosen Word — Repeated Passages

**Last updated:** 2026-10-22
**Status:** Draft — Phase 1, Loop 1

Passages Christ cited in more than one catalog entry, ranked by priority. Priority is based on **distinct citation occasions** — genuinely different settings, audiences, or purposes — not raw catalog entry count. Parallel Gospel accounts of the same event are counted as one occasion.

---

## Priority Key

| Priority | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| P1 | 4+ distinct occasions — Christ returned to this text in multiple separate settings |
| P2 | 2–3 distinct occasions — genuinely different settings or two separate sermon deliveries |
| P3 | 1 occasion, recorded in 3 parallel Gospel accounts |
| P4 | 1 occasion, recorded in 2 parallel Gospel accounts |

P1 and P2 are the most analytically significant: they indicate Christ deliberately drew from the same source more than once. P3 and P4 indicate prominence in the record but not necessarily intentional repetition.

## P1 — 4 Distinct Occasions

### RP-001 · Lev 19:18 — "Love your neighbor"
**Catalog entries:** Q-009, Q-026, Q-045, Q-078 (primary); Q-021, Q-040, Q-059 (in commandment lists)
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Antithesis 6 — Sermon on the Mount, Galilee (Q-009 / Matt 5:43)
2. Antithesis 6 — Sermon at Bountiful, 3 Nephi (Q-078 / 3 Ne 12:43)
3. The Great Commandment — response to lawyer, Jerusalem temple (Q-026 / Matt 22:39; Q-045 / Mark 12:31)
4. Rich Ruler — commandment list, Judea or Galilee (Q-021, Q-040, Q-059)

**Observation:** No other OT passage appears in as many distinct teaching contexts. Christ cites this verse across two entirely separate ministries (mortal Galilee; post-Resurrection Bountiful), in response to a hostile legal question, and in a private pastoral conversation. That breadth is unique in the catalog.

## P2 — 2 Distinct Occasions

### RP-002 · Hos 6:6 — "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice"
**Catalog entries:** Q-010, Q-014
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Pharisees criticize Jesus for eating with sinners (Q-010 / Matt 9:13)
2. Pharisees challenge Sabbath grain-picking (Q-014 / Matt 12:7)

**Observation:** The only case in the catalog where Christ returns to the same OT verse in two genuinely different disputes within a single Gospel. Both occasions are Sabbath or purity controversies with Pharisees. He quotes it directly both times with "go ye and learn what that meaneth" and "if ye had known what this meaneth" — suggesting he expected them to know the text and was calling them to account against it. This deliberate repetition within Matthew is a strong signal.

### RP-003 · Ps 110:1 — "The Lord said unto my Lord, Sit thou on my right hand"
**Catalog entries:** Q-027, Q-046, Q-063 (primary); Q-032, Q-048 (composite with Dan 7:13)
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Question about David's son — Jerusalem temple (Q-027 / Matt 22:44; Q-046 / Mark 12:36; Q-063 / Luke 20:42–43) — 3 parallel accounts of 1 occasion
2. Trial before the high priest — composite with Dan 7:13 (Q-032 / Matt 26:64; Q-048 / Mark 14:62) — 2 parallel accounts of 1 occasion

**Observation:** Christ uses this verse to make two very different points: first to establish his divine identity by posing a question the scribes cannot answer; then to make a direct claim at his trial about where he is going. The same verse serves both as an intellectual challenge and as a personal declaration.

---

### RP-004 · Dan 7:13 — "The Son of man coming in the clouds"
**Catalog entries:** Q-030, Q-064 (primary); Q-032, Q-048 (composite with Ps 110:1)
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Olivet Discourse — future coming in glory (Q-030 / Matt 24:30; Q-064 / Luke 21:27) — 2 parallel accounts of 1 occasion
2. Trial before high priest — composite with Ps 110:1 (Q-032 / Matt 26:64; Q-048 / Mark 14:62) — 2 parallel accounts of 1 occasion

**Observation:** Like Ps 110:1, Christ uses this Daniel imagery in two distinct contexts — prophecy to disciples and testimony at trial. The fact that he combines Dan 7:13 with Ps 110:1 at the trial (Q-032, Q-048) is itself significant: he is stacking two OT texts to make one integrated claim about his identity and future.

---

### RP-005 · Ps 118:26 — "Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord"
**Catalog entries:** Q-028, Q-058
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Woes to scribes and Pharisees — Jerusalem temple, Passion Week (Q-028 / Matt 23:39)
2. Lament over Jerusalem — earlier Galilean/Perean ministry (Q-058 / Luke 13:35)

**Observation:** Matt 23 is clearly during the final week in Jerusalem; Luke 13 falls earlier in Jesus's ministry. Two occasions, different audiences, different emotional registers — the Luke version is a lament; the Matthew version is a prophetic declaration. Both point forward to a future recognition Christ says has not yet happened.

### RP-006 · Exod 20:12 — "Honour thy father and mother"
**Catalog entries:** Q-017, Q-037 (tradition dispute); Q-021, Q-040, Q-059 (rich ruler list)
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Tradition dispute with Pharisees over hand-washing — cited as God's command being voided by human tradition (Q-017 / Matt 15:4; Q-037 / Mark 7:10)
2. Rich ruler — cited as part of the commandment list for eternal life (Q-021 / Matt 19:17–19; Q-040 / Mark 10:19; Q-059 / Luke 18:20)

**Observation:** Christ uses the same commandment for two very different purposes: once as a rebuke (the Pharisees are breaking it with their traditions), and once as instruction (here is what you already know you should do). Same verse, opposite rhetorical direction.

---

### RP-007 · Isa 52:8–10 — "Thy watchmen shall lift up the voice"
**Catalog entries:** Q-079, Q-082
**Distinct occasions:**
1. Christ's first day of teaching — 3 Ne 16:18–20
2. Christ's second day of teaching — 3 Ne 20:32–35

**Observation:** Christ quotes the same Isaiah 52 passage on two separate days of his Nephite visit. Worth examining whether the framing shifts between the two uses, or whether the second use is a deliberate echo to reinforce the same point.

---

### RP-008 · Isa 52:12 — "Ye shall not go out with haste"
**Catalog entries:** Q-085 (3 Ne 20:42), Q-089 (3 Ne 21:29)
**Distinct occasions:**
Both within Christ's extended address across 3 Ne 20–21. This verse appears twice within what is essentially one continuous discourse.

**Observation:** This is the only case in the catalog where Christ repeats a verse within a single continuous address. That level of repetition — even within one sermon — may indicate this particular verse carries specific doctrinal weight he wanted to anchor twice.

### RP-009 through RP-013 · The Antithesis Passages — Mosaic law cited in "ye have heard / but I say" structure

These five OT law passages each appear twice: once in the Sermon on the Mount (Galilee, Matt 5) and once in the Sermon at Bountiful (3 Nephi 12). These are **two distinct deliveries** — different audiences, different covenantal moments, separated by decades and an ocean. The same structure used twice is itself a pattern.

Note: the antitheses draw on Mosaic law broadly, not strictly the Ten Commandments. Murder and adultery are from Exod 20/Deut 5; the other four come from elsewhere in the Pentateuch.

## Summary

**27 OT passages or passage clusters** appear in more than one catalog entry (updated to include D&C section).

**D&C addition — Mal 4:1 now appears three times across the full catalog:**
- 3 Ne 25:1 (Q-092) — Malachi 4 delivered in full to Nephites
- D&C 29:9 (DC-001) — "all the proud...shall be as stubble"
- D&C 133:64 (DC-017) — near-verbatim Mal 4:1

**D&C additions — new passages unique to D&C:**
- Isa 63:1, 63:3 (DC-015, DC-016) — wine-press / atonement imagery
- Zech 13:6 (DC-009) — wounds in hands at recognition
- Isa 44:6 / 48:12 (DC-012) — "first and last" divine title


| Priority | Count | Nature |
|----------|-------|--------|
| P1 | 1 | Cited in 4 distinct ministry occasions |
| P2 | 13 | Cited in 2 distinct occasions (genuine repetitions or dual-sermon deliveries) |
| P3 | 5 | Cited once, 3 parallel Gospel accounts |
| P4 | 8 | Cited once, 2 parallel Gospel accounts |

**The strongest signal in the catalog:** Lev 19:18 (love your neighbor) is the only OT passage Christ drew from in four genuinely separate teaching contexts. Hos 6:6 (mercy not sacrifice) is the only passage he returns to twice within a single Gospel in two different disputes — suggesting it was a text he expected his audience to know and was actively applying as a diagnostic.
