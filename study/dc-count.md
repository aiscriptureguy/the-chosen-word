## Summary

- **Total D&C entries found: 17**
- **Sections containing OT quotations by Christ: D&C 29, 35, 38, 45, 61, 110, 112, 133**
- **Agent-proposed recommendation:** Include D&C with a caveat (see note below)

**Count note:** 17 entries is a meaningful addition. It falls between the "fewer than 15" and "more than 20" thresholds flagged in the research brief, closer to the upper end. D&C 45 and 133 together account for roughly half the entries and are the richest sections. The quotation density per section is lower than in the Gospels or 3 Nephi — many D&C sections use OT-style language generally without quoting a specific identifiable passage.

**Caveat on speaker identification:** The D&C is more complex than the Gospels as a text. The primary voice in most sections is identified as "the Lord" or "Jesus Christ" in section headings. However, some sections shift between narrators, angels, and Christ without clear verse-level markers. Where the speaker identification was uncertain, this is noted in the entries below. All voice identifications here are based on section-heading attribution and first-person pronoun use, not on explicit verse-level speaker labels.

**Recommendation (agent-proposed, not a conclusion):** Including D&C adds meaningful entries, especially the Isaiah 63 cluster in D&C 133 and the Ezekiel explicit citation in D&C 29:21 — the only section in the D&C where Christ names a specific OT prophet by name within the quotation itself. Including D&C would bring the catalog to roughly 108 entries. If the project's thesis is about Christ's relationship to the OT canon across dispensations, the D&C material strengthens that thesis. If the project is focused narrowly on the mortal ministry, D&C is outside scope. This decision belongs to Chase.

### Edge cases and classification questions

1. **D&C 38:3** — "I am the same which spake, and the world was made" echoes creation language but no specific OT verse is identifiable. Not counted.
2. **D&C 39:1** — "the Great I Am" is a divine title formula echoing Exodus 3:14. The existing catalog may already handle this formula in Gospel passages. If the catalog includes divine-title self-identification ("I AM") as an OT quotation type, this entry should be reconsidered for inclusion.
3. **D&C 84:22** — "no man can see the face of God, even the Father, and live" — this is a doctrinal statement that echoes Exodus 33:20 in meaning. However, Christ states it as doctrinal principle, not as a quotation. Classified as thematic echo; not counted. If Chase decides to count doctrinal-principle echoes, this would become DC-018.
4. **D&C 29:14** (Joel) — The "moon shall be turned into blood" language also appears in Acts 2:20 (Peter quoting Joel). This is an OT quotation that passed into common apocalyptic vocabulary. Christ may be echoing Joel directly or echoing the broader tradition. Counted as an OT allusion but flagged as lower confidence than DC-004.
5. **D&C 133:40–46** — These verses use language very close to Isaiah 64:1 ("O that thou wouldst rend the heavens"). However, the speaker in verses 40–46 is the narrative voice or an intercessory voice addressing the Lord, not Christ speaking in first person. Christ's first-person response begins at verse 47. Not counted for verses 40–46.
6. **D&C 133:64 vs. D&C 29:9** — Both closely echo Malachi 4:1. This is the same OT source appearing twice in different sections. Each is counted separately as a D&C entry but would count as one OT source in a by-book tally.
